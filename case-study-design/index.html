Designing
Magical Chart

Every visual and editorial decision in Magical Chart was made to serve one goal: a space that feels safe enough to be honest in. Here is how the universe was built, and why each choice was made the way it was.

01 · The constraint

Not mystical. Not clinical. Something in between.

Tarot and astrology products usually go one of two directions: dark and esoteric (crystal energy, moon rituals, dramatic fonts), or stripped-back and rational (data, charts, cold UI). Both feel wrong for what Magical Chart is.

The brief I gave myself: a night universe — dreamy, calm, immersive — that makes reading feel natural, not performative. The spectacle should be the content, not the interface.

"Not a guru in spirituality. A space where you can be honest with yourself — and read without distraction."

02 · The background

Why dark navy, not black or white

The question

Why not a white or parchment background — the standard for editorial and "calm" products?

The decision

White reads as clinical. Parchment reads as vintage occult. Dark navy is the sky at night — it creates the dreamy, immersive feeling without signalling danger or mysticism.

The question

Why not pure black — the standard for "premium dark mode"?

The decision

Black feels cold and contracted. Navy has depth — it suggests infinity, the cosmos, the feeling of lying on the grass and looking up. The gradient radials reinforce this: each page has its own subtle glow, like stars in different positions.

03 · The accent colour

One colour for everything that matters

The constraint was: dark navy universe, minimal colour, maximum contrast. Too many colours and the night feeling breaks.

Cyan #00e5ff was chosen because it reads as starlight — it has the right luminosity against dark navy without warming the palette toward orange or red, which would shift the mood toward fire, urgency, warning. Cyan stays cold and clear: the light of the sky at 4am, not the light of a candle.

The rule

Cyan is used for one thing: what deserves attention right now.

The result

Links, active states, the most important number on a screen. Never decorative. The eye follows it automatically — which means it has to earn its use every time.

04 · Typography

A typeface for reading, not for looking at

Cormorant Garamond for headings and reading content. Plus Jakarta Sans for UI labels, metadata, small text. The contrast between the two is intentional: the serif carries the content, the sans carries the structure.

The question

Why Cormorant Garamond and not a more contemporary serif — or no serif at all?

The decision

Cormorant is elegant without being precious. It has the feeling of a book you read by a lamp at night — the right register for tarot interpretations and astrological reports. Modern serifs (like Playfair) have too much display personality; they draw attention to themselves. Cormorant recedes and lets the words carry the weight.

The constraint

Users come to read long interpretations — sometimes 500-1,000 words. The typography has to support that.

The rule

No fantasy fonts, no decorative display type in body copy. Ornamentation belongs to the mascot, not to the letters. Reading comfort comes first.

05 · The mascot

Why a sheep, and why this sheep

Wooly is a small sheep floating on a cloud above a starry sky. Round, soft, slightly luminous. The design decisions were entirely guided by what the product needs to feel like.

Why a sheep

Sheep are associated with dreams — counting sheep, drifting off. The mascot had to live in the liminal space between waking and sleeping, where you let yourself think about what you actually feel. A sheep on a cloud does that without explanation.

Why round and soft

Tarot is emotionally vulnerable territory. People come with real questions about relationships, decisions, doubts. A sharp, angular or "cool" character would create distance. Wooly is approachable because the product needs you to feel safe being honest.

Why not a human or a mystical figure

A human character would imply authority — a guru, a reader, someone who knows things you don't. That is exactly the dynamic the product rejects. A mascot has no power over you. It reflects; it doesn't judge.

Why Wooly is the only ornament

The UI has no illustrations, no decorative elements, no crystal balls or pentagram borders. All the visual personality is concentrated in Wooly. This keeps the reading content primary — the mascot is the warmth, the rest is the space to think.

06 · Brand voice

What Wooly says and doesn't say

The visual direction and the editorial voice were designed together. A dreamy, non-judgmental universe needs a voice that matches — which meant defining what Wooly would never say as much as what it would.

Never

"The cards predict…" / "Your destiny is…" / "The universe is telling you…"

Always

"The cards reflect…" / "This might be worth sitting with…" / "Wooly wonders if…"

The rule: Wooly mirrors, it doesn't tell. Every reading ends with the user's own agency intact. The visual softness and the editorial restraint are the same decision in two different forms.

"It doesn't feel manipulative — you actually know what you're signing up for."
