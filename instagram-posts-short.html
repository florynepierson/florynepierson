Reels — expériences vécues

Overlay
my analytics were broken for 5 days. I had no idea.
Légende
a 2s setTimeout on 1,500+ pages = most of my traffic invisible to GA4 🫠 the tag was there. the script loaded. it just fired too early. I was making product decisions on data that didn't exist. lesson: check *when* your analytics fires, not just whether it's installed save this if you run your own site 🔖
Overlay
my Stripe webhook was failing 100% of the time. zero error logs.
Légende
paying users weren't getting access. nothing looked wrong. the bug: I was sending 200 *before* verifying the signature Stripe marks it successful the moment it gets a 200 — it doesn't care what happens after fix: verify first. process second. respond third. the scariest bugs are the ones that don't throw errors 🔥
Overlay
50 fake sessions per day. GA4 didn't catch one.
Légende
headless Chrome scrapers running real JS, real events, real session data 🤖 GA4's bot filter works on user agents. these had clean ones. the tell: screen dimensions 1280×1200 and 1366×1366 — no real device has those one check at the top of your analytics script = problem solved your data might be lying to you rn 👀 save this 🔖
Overlay
I went from 1,500 SEO pages to 22. rankings went up.
Légende
counterintuitive but it makes sense when you think about it 264 near-duplicate pages merged → 22 rich ones less pages competing for the same query cleaner crawl budget stronger topical focus per page more pages ≠ more SEO more pages for the same intent = self-sabotage save if you do content SEO 🔖
Overlay
Google sends me the most traffic. zero paying users.
Légende
direct traffic converts. Google doesn't. why: Google intent = curiosity. direct intent = return. someone searched "free tarot reading" → they want free someone came back directly → they already liked what they found traffic and purchase intent are different things I almost optimised for the wrong one for months 😬 what's your highest traffic source that doesn't convert? 👇
Overlay
I sent hundreds of cold messages. almost no one replied.
Légende
cold outreach without an audience is just noise you're asking for attention from people who have no reason to trust you yet what actually worked: building in public, documenting what I measured, making the work visible inbounds are slower to start. they compound. build the audience first. then outreach becomes a conversation, not a pitch 📌 save this 🔖
Overlay
the first time a stranger paid for my product, I didn't celebrate.
Légende
I opened the database to check the webhook had fired correctly that's when I knew I was an engineer 😂 the celebration came after: row exists ✓ access granted ✓ email sent ✓ building a product means your first instinct after a sale is to verify the system worked there's something satisfying about that tbh what was your reaction to your first sale? 👇
Overlay
nobody tells you when you're doing well as a solo founder.
Légende
no manager. no team. no sprint review. you learn to read data the way other people read feedback open rate up → something resonated conversion flat → there's a hole in the funnel churn spike → something broke the trust the data doesn't validate you. it tells you what's working. that's actually more useful. solo founders: what's your signal that something is working? 👇
Overlay
I built for a market I didn't belong to.
Légende
I'm not a tarot reader. I'm an engineer who measured what people were searching for. you don't have to be the user. you have to understand them well enough to build something they'd choose. the advantage of being an outsider: you question things insiders take for granted what market are you building for? 👇
Overlay
my AI persona has a scoring rubric. 6 dimensions. 60 points.
Légende
context use · source grounding · guardrails · language match · persona coherence · concrete grounding baseline: 55/60 after two targeted prompt fixes: 60/60 you can't improve what you don't measure — including personality the model doesn't have a personality by default. you define it, then you score it. save if you're building LLM products 🔖
Overlay
my AI product costs ~$0.005 per reading. I still didn't optimise that first.
Légende
gross margin: ~99% optimising AI cost would have saved me a few dollars a month what I optimised instead: the funnel. where users drop between reading and purchase. that's where the revenue was hiding. instrument before you optimise. the bottleneck is almost never the most technical thing. save this 🔖
Overlay
slow growth is underrated.
Légende
I didn't go viral. organic growth over months, one decision at a time. what slow growth gives you that viral doesn't: → you learn what's actually working → your infra scales with users, not ahead of them → your audience chose you deliberately viral is a moment. compounding is a business. the goal isn't a spike. it's a slope that doesn't flatten 📈

Tips — business & acquisition

Overlay
if your discount isn't converting, the problem isn't the price.
Légende
desire can't be fixed with a promo code discount = price sensitivity test no movement = they don't want it enough yet fix: improve what you're offering before you cut the price also: urgency converts. discount alone doesn't. "50% off" → barely moved "50% off — ends tonight" → meaningfully better same discount. deadline made the difference. save this 🔖
Overlay
pick one acquisition channel first. master it. then diversify.
Légende
every channel has one job: Pinterest → curiosity about your universe Google → capture existing intent Instagram → trust through face & voice X → credibility in your niche TikTok → reach people who didn't know they needed you mixing these up without knowing what you're asking each one to do = wasting all of them you don't diversify until the first channel actually works what's your main channel rn? 👇
Overlay
capture email after the experience. never before.
Légende
asking for the email before giving anything = the user has zero proof you're worth their inbox the right moment: right after they've received something valuable and are still in it users who experienced the product before seeing a price converted at a much higher rate than those who hit a wall first the gate comes after the experience. always. save this 🔖
Overlay
your free tier needs to be real. or the paid tier doesn't earn itself.
Légende
if you need to weaken the free tier to sell the paid one, the paid one isn't good enough yet the upgrade should be obvious on its own: "I want more of this" ← this is the goal "I guess I have to pay to continue" ← this is a gate, not a product the free experience is the acquisition mechanism. not a cost. what does your free tier actually give users? 👇
Overlay
before you optimise anything: instrument first.
Légende
you can't fix what you can't see most people optimise the thing that feels most technical. it's rarely the actual bottleneck. measure: where do users drop? what did they do right before they left? what did paying users do differently? the data will point at something unexpected every time then optimise that thing. not the one that felt obvious. save this 🔖
Overlay
same product, same price. different countries, different conversion.
Légende
$10 means something very different in France, India and Brazil purchasing power parity is real and it affects willingness to pay but it's not just PPP — intent differs too high traffic from a country ≠ high intent to pay from that country check: what do users from each country actually *do* on your product? before you celebrate traffic from a new market, look at what they do after they arrive 👀

Tips — AI & tools

Overlay
AI accelerates execution. it doesn't replace product sense.
Légende
AI suggested features I didn't build, optimisations that didn't matter, abstractions I didn't need every suggestion was technically correct I had to decide whether "technically correct" was actually useful the judgment — what to build vs what to skip — doesn't come from the model that comes from understanding your users, your data, your constraints the skill isn't using AI. it's knowing when to say no to what it suggests. what's the most useful AI tool in your stack? 👇
Overlay
use AI for the first version of every design. refine after.
Légende
AI → 80% of the design in an hour fast enough to test · good enough to ship · cheap enough to throw away the last 20% — taste, consistency, details users feel but can't name — that's where Figma/Canva/a designer earns their cost rule: don't polish what hasn't been validated yet ship the AI version → learn what's worth refining → then spend the design budget save this 🔖
Overlay
cheaper model ≠ worse product. it depends what you're using it for.
Légende
Haiku (cheapest): classification, rewriting, simple generation, repetitive tasks ✅ Sonnet (mid): coding, reasoning, complex generation ✅ Opus (most expensive): deep reasoning, nuanced judgment ✅ most people default to the most powerful model for everything that's like using a sledgehammer to hang a picture frame match the model to the task. your cost drops, your latency drops, nothing breaks. save this if you're building with LLMs 🔖
Overlay
how to tell if an email opportunity is a scam.
Légende
🚩 Gmail/Yahoo sender, not a company domain 🚩 no website in the signature 🚩 vague about what they actually need 🚩 "urgent" with no real deadline 🚩 payment upfront before any contract 🚩 username changed recently, low followers none of these alone is conclusive all of them together is a pattern any serious business emails you from their own domain. that's the first filter. save this 🔖
Overlay
AI as a feature vs AI as the product.
Légende
AI as a feature: "we added AI to summarise your notes" AI as the product: remove the AI and there's nothing left the products that will last are the ones where the AI makes the core experience irreplaceable — not just cheaper or faster when AI is the product, every decision flows from one question: does this make the AI experience better for the user? which category is your product in? 👇

Tips — Blockchain & Web3

Overlay
what a smart contract actually does. in plain English.
Légende
code that lives on a blockchain and runs automatically when conditions are met no middleman. no "our team will review." no processing time. classic example: escrow normally you need a bank or lawyer to hold funds until both parties deliver a smart contract does the same — rules written in code, visible to everyone, executes automatically "trustless" means you trust the code, not the other person the catch: writing that code correctly is extremely hard. bugs are irreversible. save this 🔖
Overlay
Web3 UX is broken. everyone in the industry knows it.
Légende
seed phrases: 12 random words. lose them = lose everything forever. no recovery. gas fees: send $5, pay $3 in unpredictable fees irreversible transactions: sent to the wrong address? gone. the technical foundation is solid the UX layer on top is still treating normal users like cryptographers the products that win in Web3 won't have the most sophisticated contracts they'll make this usable by someone who's never read a whitepaper that's an engineering + product problem. which means it's solvable 👀
Overlay
decentralisation is a spectrum, not a binary.
Légende
"is it decentralised?" is usually the wrong question better questions: → who controls the smart contracts? can they be upgraded? → who controls the frontend? if the site goes down, can you still access funds? → who owns the majority of governance tokens? most "decentralised" protocols are decentralised on some dimensions and centralised on others that's not necessarily bad — full decentralisation has real costs but knowing where the centralisation lives tells you where the real risks are save this if you're evaluating Web3 projects 🔖
Overlay
token vs coin. the actual difference.
Légende
coin: has its own blockchain → Bitcoin (Bitcoin blockchain), Ether (Ethereum), SOL (Solana) token: runs on top of an existing blockchain → USDC (runs on Ethereum), most DeFi tokens why it matters: coins = native currency of a network, used to pay gas fees tokens = built by anyone using smart contracts, represent anything (governance rights, stablecoins, NFTs...) most things called "coins" in the news are actually tokens save this if you're new to crypto 🔖

Tips — Tech & Engineering

Overlay
log everything that should happen. alert on silence.
Légende
most monitoring is reactive: alert when something throws an error the problem: the worst bugs don't throw errors. they just quietly don't work. better approach: log every expected event → webhook received ✓ → database write complete ✓ → email sent ✓ if any of those stops appearing in logs, something is wrong — even if nothing errored silence is a signal. treat it like one. save this 🔖
Overlay
a project works when you're watching. a product works when you're not.
Légende
the gap between the two: → monitoring for things that should happen but don't → graceful failure when an external API goes down → logs you can actually read at 11pm on a Sunday building something that runs without you watching is a different skill than building something that works most tutorials teach you the second one save this if you're moving from side projects to real products 🔖
Overlay
measure the broken state before you fix anything.
Légende
I had a bug in my astronomy engine. error of 0.29°. before touching a single line of code: I used Swiss Ephemeris as a reference and measured exactly how wrong I was, on what inputs, by how much. then I fixed it. 0.29° → 0.003°. the rule: never touch the code before you know what "correct" looks like fixing without measuring first = guessing measuring first = you know exactly when you're done save this 🔖
Overlay
every function should do exactly one thing.
Légende
when a function starts doing two things, debugging is a guessing game when it does three, you've lost track of what it's responsible for this isn't just for large teams — it's how one person keeps a production system understandable at 2am when something breaks my rule: if I can't describe what a function does in one sentence, it does too much what's the most useful engineering principle you actually apply? 👇
